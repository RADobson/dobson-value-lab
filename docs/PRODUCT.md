# Value Lab: sales concept and next experiments

## The proposition

"Before you buy more technology, make the value measurable."

Dobson Development can use Value Lab as an open-source trust builder and discovery tool. A prospective client brings their own numbers, sees the assumptions behind the result and leaves with a business case that can be tested. No email gate is required to use the calculator.

## A useful first client conversation

1. Select one business outcome: reduce a defined loss exposure, recover time in a repeatable workflow, or remove an actual expense.
2. Establish a baseline using the client's own incident history, work sampling, invoices or contracts.
3. Enter the full implementation and operating costs, including internal effort, review, exceptions and delay.
4. Stress the case. Discuss the condition that would make it unattractive.
5. Export a brief naming the decision owner, largest uncertainty and pilot success measure.
6. Agree a bounded pilot. Measure before and after; update evidence rather than treating the initial estimate as proof.

The commercial next step is an evidence-backed assessment or pilot, not a promise of a particular ROI. No unverified contact details, booking links or pricing claims are embedded in this initial release.

## Useful metrics by buyer

| Buyer / question | Metric | Supporting evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Executive / where should we invest? | Net value, value/cost, downside result, time to value | Full cost and scenario assumptions |
| Cyber leader / what risk remains? | Baseline and residual annual expected loss; loss avoided per dollar | Loss scenario definition, frequency/impact ranges, control testing |
| Operations / will people get time back? | Net hours recovered per $1,000 | Work sampling including review, exceptions and adoption |
| Finance / what expense will stop? | Realised cash savings per dollar, cash after cost | Budget owner confirmation and identifiable expense lines |
| Delivery / can we realise it? | Go-live delay, evidence level, pilot acceptance threshold | Accountable owner, instrumentation and review date |

## Candidate next modules

These are product ideas, not implemented features.

- **Pilot actuals:** baseline versus observed results, adoption, exception rate, quality and realised costs.
- **Service quality:** error/rework rate, first-pass success and customer response time. Monetise only with supportable data.
- **Revenue capacity:** incremental contribution margin, with conversion and capacity constraints; never treat revenue as profit.
- **Resilience:** recovery time, outage hours avoided, recovery testing coverage and worst-case exposure.
- **Dependency-aware portfolios:** overlapping risk controls, shared delivery costs and budget constraints.
- **Probabilistic risk analysis:** explicit input distributions and sensitivity attribution, reviewed by a qualified risk practitioner; avoid implying that a simple 5×5 heatmap supplies a dollar value.
- **A verified sales handoff:** business contact or booking destination, with an explicit user action before transmitting a client brief.
- **Brand integration:** adopt the company's approved visual identity and custom domain when supplied.

## Initial scope decisions

- Public MIT-licensed code and a separately controlled ChatGPT Sites deployment.
- Browser-local calculations, no analytics or client-data collection.
- Three independent economic models, not a misleading combined score.
- Clear examples and evidence capture, not unsupported industry benchmarks.
- A downloadable brief as the sales artefact, with no automated outreach.

## Suggested success measures for the tool itself

In a small, consented usability test, measure completion time, whether decision makers can explain the assumptions, whether they distinguish cash from capacity, and whether the brief produces a specific pilot decision. Add tracking only if the business deliberately chooses a privacy approach.
